Moon panel, take two

A’s big Moon, plain rows and calendar look, with an Apple-style ruler and calendar. Real Kansas City data for October through December 2026. Drag the ruler, scroll over it or the Moon, or click it and use the arrow keys. Tap any night in the calendar, and use the arrows to change months.

Why does the Moon change shape?The Sun always lights half of the Moon. As it circles Earth, we see more or less of that lit half.

What changed from A

  • Tonight is shown with teal, never with words: the date line, the ruler label and the calendar number.
  • The ruler is a ruler of nights. Tall ticks are at noon, so 2am Friday still belongs to Thursday night, like the Tonight page.
  • The hour you’re looking at rides on the yellow pointer.
  • A teal arrow appears beside the title once you move away: on the left when you’ve gone later, on the right when you’ve gone earlier. It slides back to midnight tonight, the middle notch of tonight.
  • The panel opens at midnight tonight. Tapping a night in the calendar also goes to that night’s midnight.
  • Notches are every 2 hours. The mouse wheel and trackpad move one notch at a time, and the yellow label always shows the notch you’ll land on.
  • Next full Moon says “5 days” within a week, “Tomorrow” or “Today” when very close, and just the date otherwise.
  • Calendar numbers sit above each Moon. Tapping a night jumps there instantly, with no sliding, and keeps the time of night you were on; the arrows only change which month you’re browsing. The teal arrow and Home jump instantly too.

Keyboard

  • On the ruler: ← → move a night, Shift + ← → move one notch, Home jumps back to tonight. Fast presses always land exactly on the next notch.
